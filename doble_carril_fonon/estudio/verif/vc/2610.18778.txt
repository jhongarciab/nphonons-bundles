arXiv:2610.18778v1 [quant-ph] 13 Oct 2026




                                                         within a single compact device, making them attractive for quantum simulation, quantum infor-
                                                         mation processing, and quantum-enhanced sensing. Many such applications rely on multipartite
                                                         entanglement, yet deterministic mechanical entanglement has so far been largely limited to pairs of
                                                         modes. Here we demonstrate the deterministic and programmable preparation of multipartite en-
                                                         tangled states among the phonon modes of a high-overtone bulk acoustic wave resonator coupled to
                                                         a superconducting transmon qubit. By sequentially applying optimal-control-engineered isometries
                                                         between the qubit and successive modes, we generate matrix product states of bond dimension two,
                                                         including W , Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger (GHZ), and cluster states. We fully characterize these
                                                         states via quantum state tomography and certify genuine multipartite entanglement among up to
                                                         four modes. We further demonstrate correlated-noise sensing by exploiting the complementary de-
                                                         coherence responses of the stored W and GHZ states, isolating inter-mode phase correlations from
                                                         local decoherence and placing quantitative bounds on collective dephasing. Our results establish a
                                                         scalable approach to preparing multipartite entangled states in mechanical systems, with promising
                                                         applications in multiparameter sensing and tests of quantum mechanics with massive objects.

                               1.   Advantage of isometry synthesis over unitary synthesis

  The essential distinction between the present optimization and fully specified unitary synthesis is the number of
constrained input columns. A specified unitary fixes the evolution of an entire basis, whereas the isometry fixes
only the D = 2 columns associated with the physically populated input subspace Hin . The other Nh − D columns,
represented by B1′ and B2′ in Eq. (S18), remain free. This enlarges the manifold of acceptable unitary extensions
and gives the optimizer additional freedom to find controls that can be shorter or easier to realize than pulses for a
particular fully specified unitary, such as a chosen CNOT extension [81, 82].
